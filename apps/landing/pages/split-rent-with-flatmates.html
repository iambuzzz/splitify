How to split rent and bills with flatmates

Rent is the biggest bill you’ll share, and the one most likely to cause quiet resentment if the rooms aren’t the same. Here are three fair ways to split it, and how to handle everything else a flat costs.

Updated 27 October 2026 · 6 minute read

Option 1: Split the rent equally

Simple and best when the rooms are about the same. ₹45,000 rent for three people is ₹15,000 each. If one room has an attached bathroom or a balcony and the others don’t, equal starts to feel unfair, and that’s when to use one of the next two options.

Option 2: Split by room size

Each person pays in proportion to their room’s area. Measure the rooms (or use the sizes from the floor plan) and divide.

RoomSizePays
Room with bathroom160 sq ft₹18,947
Middle room120 sq ft₹14,211

This can feel harsh, though: everyone uses the kitchen, the hall and the balcony equally, and that space isn’t counted at all.

Option 3: Split common space equally, rooms by size

The fairest option for most flats. Decide what part of the rent pays for shared space (40% is a reasonable starting point), split that equally, and split the rest by room size.

Rounded to the rupee (the leftover rupee goes to the biggest room), these add up to exactly ₹45,000. Agree on the split once, write it down, and use the same numbers every month.

Electricity, Wi-Fi, cook and groceries

  • Wi-Fi, cook, cleaning, gas: split equally. Everyone benefits about the same.
  • Electricity: equally, unless one person runs an AC all night and the others don’t. Then agree on a fixed extra amount for them rather than measuring every month.
  • Groceries and household items: whoever buys adds the bill. Leave out anyone who opted out of shared food.
  • Deposit: record who paid how much of it. When it comes back, it goes back the same way.

Settle once a month

Different people pay different things: one pays the landlord, one pays the electricity, one buys groceries. Don’t pay each other back bill by bill. Once a month, add up everyone’s balance (what they paid minus their share) and settle in as few UPI payments as possible. The split bill calculator does this from a list of who paid what.

When someone moves in or out mid-month

Split that month’s rent by days. If a flatmate leaves on the 10th of a 30-day month, they pay 10/30 of their usual share and the new person (or everyone else) covers the remaining 20 days. Settle their balance on the day they move out, before the deposit is returned.

Questions

What’s the fairest way to split rent with different room sizes?

Split a part of the rent for common space equally (around 40%) and the rest by room size. It accounts for the bigger room without ignoring the kitchen and hall everyone shares.

Should the person with the attached bathroom pay more?

Usually yes. Either count the bathroom in their room size, or agree on a fixed extra amount each month, like ₹1,000, and take it off everyone else’s share.

How often should flatmates settle up?

Once a month works for most flats: right after rent is paid, so all the month’s bills are in.
